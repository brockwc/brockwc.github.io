I spent the weekend getting my tooling in order. I revived my personal website (a Jekyll blog that had been sitting since 2015) and restructured it as a user site at brockwc.github.io, with a categories page and a place to log weekly project posts. I also built a deadline tracker and a weekly update workflow so I don't lose track of deliverables over the term. I don't think I have the UI to access those pages set up yet but that will be my next step.

On the project itself: I went through the sample ideas page and the recent student projects. The ideas that keep pulling me are the ones where the technology is new to me. I work in Go and SQL, so I'm weighing two directions: building a 2D roguelike in Rust (procedural map generation, pathfinding, a small combat system), or building software (a childcare center management tool) for my wife's business that could replace what she uses and pays for now. As if I didn't have enough to think about, I am also considering a project focused on setting up a homelab where I could experiment with new technologies and learn a little more about hardware, networking, and security. I am kind of excited about this one but I'm not sure if it is the right fit. I've been asking myself what I think I will still want to work on in week 8, and I think I am leaning towards one of the ideas that would force me to learn Rust, because I've never written more than toy programs in it, my job may ultimately go in that direction, and the learning curve is the point.

No blockers. The proposal goes up next week, and after that the real work starts.
